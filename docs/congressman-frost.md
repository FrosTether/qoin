## The paradox, stated first

A basic income paid by a government is paid with taxes, and taxes are collected
under threat of force. An anarcho-capitalist can't square that: a good end does
not launder a coerced means. So "an ancap running for Congress to deliver UBI"
looks like a contradiction — and it is, if the plan is to *legislate* an income.

That is not the plan. The plan is to prove that a universal basic income does not
need a tax or a check from anyone's pocket — that it can be **issued by a rule and
secured by work**, adopted by people who choose it, and paid to every verified
human without a dollar taken from anyone who didn't opt in.

**The plan now includes one law.** Qoin as a universal basic income depends on the
proposed Jacob Frost Blockchain Bill Act becoming law. See [The bill](#the-bill).

"Congressman Frost" runs, if he runs at all, for the one honest reason an ancap
can: to hold the megaphone, pass the Jacob Frost Blockchain Bill Act, repeal
what's coercive, and make the office unnecessary. The goal is exit, not power. You
win by making the seat obsolete.

## Proof of Work of UBI — the core idea

Two kinds of work replace the tax collector.

**1. Machine work secures the money.** Every currency creates new units and hands
them to someone first. Under fiat it's banks and the state; under Bitcoin it's
miners. A proof-of-work ledger can secure an honest, unchangeable issuance rule
that no legislature can raise before an election or cut after one. The work isn't
the point — it's the lock on the rule.

**2. Human work earns the dividend.** The rule issues an equal share of new money
to every participating person, every day: the universal dividend, `D = c·M/N`
(each member gets a fixed fraction `c` of the money supply `M`, split across `N`
members). The "work" a person does is the only work that should entitle anyone to
an equal share: **proving they are one real, living human** — proof of personhood,
not proof of wealth, citizenship, or need.

Put together: *you prove you're a unique human; the network's work secures a money
whose rule pays every verified human an equal daily dividend, forever, by an
equation nobody can change.* That is a basic income you never voted for and were
never taxed for. It is redistribution with the coercion removed — a flat draw on
the money's own seigniorage, shared out equally, among people who all agreed to
the rule before they joined.

This is the thesis of the Qoinchain white paper, made into a platform.

## Why this is the ancap position, not a betrayal of it

- **Voluntary.** No one is forced to hold the currency, fund it, or receive it.
  The dividend touches only the balances of people who chose in, and every one of
  them could read the rule first.
- **No monopoly, no legal tender.** It competes for adoption against every other
  money on its merits — Hayek's competition in currencies, applied to the rules
  themselves. If people don't want it, it dies, and it should.
- **Exit over voice.** No on-chain vote can change `c`. Disagree with the
  parameters and the remedy is a fork and your feet, not a ballot that binds
  dissenters.
- **It shrinks the state's excuse.** The strongest argument for the welfare
  bureaucracy is "who will help people otherwise?" A working voluntary dividend is
  the answer that removes the excuse — and with it, a reason the apparatus exists.

Charity stays charity: voluntary, personal, admirable. The dividend is the
unconditional floor underneath it, funded by dilution nobody was forced to accept.

## The platform (what Congressman Frost stands for)

Every plank is a market or voluntary mechanism, phrased plainly:

1. **A basic income by equation, not by agency.** Back and popularize a
   voluntary, rule-issued universal dividend. Zero new taxes; zero new
   bureaucracy.
2. **End the coercive substitutes.** Wherever a voluntary floor can do the job,
   campaign to repeal the tax-funded, means-tested, poverty-trap programs it
   replaces — not to abandon people, but to stop financing help at gunpoint.
3. **Sound rules over discretionary hands.** An issuance rule fixed at genesis
   beats a central bank's discretion. Transparency by default; no privileged
   mint, no freeze, no blacklist.
4. **One human, one share.** Proof of personhood that is open, pseudonymous, and
   owned by no central registry — never a national ID or a biometric database as
   the price of a livelihood.
5. **Your keys, your income.** The dividend lands in a wallet only its holder
   controls. No account anyone else can seize, reverse, or switch off.
6. **Honesty as policy.** No promises of price or profit, no "backed" claims
   without reserves, no hype the code can't cash. The idea wins on being true.

## The hard parts, named honestly

A plan that hides its problems isn't a plan.

- **Personhood is the whole game.** If fake identities collect the dividend, it
  leaks value in proportion to the fraud. Every UBI-currency before this one found
  counting humans harder than the money. Solve it small before scaling.
- **Demand, not issuance, is the constraint.** The equation guarantees a *share*,
  not a dollar value. The dividend is worth roughly 9.5% of the average member's
  *real* holdings per year — and only if people actually want the money. Say so.
- **The name.** "FrosTether" borrows Tether's likeness; "Frost Platinum Dollar"
  implies backing that doesn't exist. Both invite a fight and a false impression.
  Rename before going public.
- **Running for office is regulated.** A real candidacy triggers election law,
  campaign-finance rules, and disclosure — and mixing a token with a campaign
  raises securities and ethics questions fast. If it goes past persona to
  candidacy, get counsel first. The manifesto is free speech; a launch is not.
- **It now waits on a vote.** Qoin as a universal basic income depends on the
  Jacob Frost Blockchain Bill Act becoming law. Until it passes, the dividend is a
  design, not an income.
- **The tension never fully dissolves.** Using the state's stage to argue against
  the state is a compromise. Own it, keep the actual mechanism voluntary, and let
  the results — not the title — do the arguing.
